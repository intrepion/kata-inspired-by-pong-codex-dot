2026-11-13, Omarchy ARM64. It strengthens artifact identity and source-recipe evidence; it is not a reproducible-build attestation, a complete notice audit or policy clearance.

## Official distribution identity

The official Rust 1.99.1 ARM64 musl `rust-std` `.tar.xz` was downloaded to the isolated audit directory. Its SHA-256 `2f3eb0d0610e2b7f6b2121d106e2490ac648decfa0c09b30873bdfc60de6c1ab` matches the installed toolchain's channel manifest. All 14 sysroot archives and four CRT objects named in the executable's linker map were read from that distribution and compared byte-for-byte with the installed files; all 18 matched. The [sanitized comparison](ratatui-musl-rust-distribution-objects-20261113.json), SHA-256 `f5099346daed2e82d9df99934f5761dee747235aa40fcb006dd249a4830f4522`, records their hashes. This establishes identity with that distribution, not how the distribution was built.

The matching official ARM64 GNU `rustc` distribution SHA-256 `acdcdfe09ae1f103aa9a2b7f88903a9588bc2e99288af10e6fbf5514e2e49f25` matched its channel-manifest value. The installed `rust-lld` and `COPYRIGHT-library.html` matched their corresponding distribution members byte-for-byte; their hashes are `9fa5cc798c03e61a0a309ec3e16069fca5bb31cb589993da7b8fb132f17d526c` and `5647be074c8edf7339fd863055923a8fc80bc5610a8d4661ec3b767b9d392c27`. The linker is a build tool, not an archive shown as linked into the application.

## Candidate libc and unwind sources

The [Rust 1.99.1 source recipe](https://github.com/rust-lang/rust/blob/58cf8f9c35b355199727627d07ae34a4e2846d67/src/ci/docker/scripts/musl-toolchain.sh) pins `musl-cross-make` revision `3635262e4524c991552789af6f36211a335a77b3`, musl 1.2.5 and two Rust-supplied patches. The pinned [Makefile](https://github.com/richfelker/musl-cross-make/blob/3635262e4524c991552789af6f36211a335a77b3/Makefile) specifies GCC 9.4.0, binutils 2.44 and musl 1.2.5. The official musl 1.2.5 source archive's SHA-1 matches the recipe's [pinned SHA-1](https://github.com/richfelker/musl-cross-make/blob/3635262e4524c991552789af6f36211a335a77b3/hashes/musl-1.2.5.tar.gz.sha1); its separately computed SHA-256 is `a9a118bbe84d8764da0ea0d28b3ab3fae8477fc7e4085d90102b8596fc7c75e4`. Its `COPYRIGHT` states the musl project as a whole uses MIT and has SHA-256 `f9bc4423732350eb0b3f7ed7e91d530298476f8fec0c6c427a1c04ade22655af`. SHA-1 matching is historical recipe evidence, not a strong independent integrity attestation. The two recipe patch blobs are `9b39420ea3c5103e1bdc43f18438f3b35366e8ce` and `88298ca471d38b2c8861f817caf394a591fcdc26`; see the [second source patch](https://github.com/rust-lang/rust/blob/58cf8f9c35b355199727627d07ae34a4e2846d67/src/ci/docker/scripts/musl-cve-2026-40200.diff). These sources have not been reproducibly built into the installed `libc.a`.

Git metadata for Rust source revision `58cf8f9c35b355199727627d07ae34a4e2846d67` identifies `src/llvm-project` as submodule commit `1b9c0d5ff9bbe7634aead059efe6b11a7eeba145` in `rust-lang/llvm-project`. Rust's [bootstrap code](https://github.com/rust-lang/rust/blob/58cf8f9c35b355199727627d07ae34a4e2846d67/src/bootstrap/src/core/build_steps/compile.rs) describes copying an LLVM `libunwind.a` into the self-contained musl sysroot. All eight matching candidate unwind source filenames at that exact submodule commit declare `Apache-2.0 WITH LLVM-exception`; see its [license file](https://github.com/rust-lang/llvm-project/blob/1b9c0d5ff9bbe7634aead059efe6b11a7eeba145/libunwind/LICENSE.TXT). Five corresponding object member names appear in the executable's link map. Source names and recipes corroborate the archive's likely origin, but do not establish a reproducible source-to-object match or exhaust included headers/third-party material.

A selected libc object and several unwind objects record compiler identity `GCC: (GNU) 9.4.0`, consistent with the candidate recipe. This is not evidence that `libgcc` code was linked or that GPL applies to those objects. The final map has no named libgcc archive, and the ELF has no dynamic section; compiler-builtins and unwind source-level/notice review remains necessary. Build-tool licensing is separate from the static components delivered in a binary.

## Linked libc member and notice candidates

The retained linker map (SHA-256 `d26cedb28004ab7735a1ede1584de5fd3fab03037c91f66366fdbdd03f27b3cd`) names 186 distinct members of the distribution `libc.a`. A filename-only scan of the pinned musl 1.2.5 archive found at least one same-basename source candidate for each member; 18 members have multiple candidates. The [candidate index](ratatui-musl-libc-member-candidates-20261113.json), SHA-256 `53e32a7e9ab14805b2529378fa79b167016bd5604db1d79788b086487afa0af0`, retains all architecture variants rather than guessing which was built. Member names and candidate matches do not prove object identity, include headers or establish the exact applied patches.

The map includes `__rem_pio2_large.lo`; the same-named `src/math/__rem_pio2_large.c` candidate contains a Sun Microsystems copyright notice and permission conditioned on preserving that notice. The source archive's `COPYRIGHT` identifies additional third-party math and AArch64 string contributions and describes the musl-wide MIT terms and an exception for specified headers and CRT files. These are concrete notice candidates for a distribution review, not an exhaustive notice list or proof that every candidate source was compiled. Preserve the original full notices and review the actual inputs, patches and any incorporated third-party material before distributing the binary; do not replace them with this summary.

## Broad Rust copyright inventory

A keyword screen of the matching `COPYRIGHT-library.html` found four entries whose declared expressions mention a restricted family: `fortanix-sgx-abi 0.6.1` (MPL-2.0) and three `r-efi`/`r-efi-alloc` entries with MIT or Apache-2.0 alternatives to LGPL. The [sanitized screen](rust-stdlib-copyright-screen-20261113.json), SHA-256 `2cdc1cb486e6a79d23bc575bccf8686880207755f59d9ccad8676e31359f2584`, is a broad Rust library inventory across targets, not the linked application's dependency list. The [Rust std manifest](https://github.com/rust-lang/rust/blob/58cf8f9c35b355199727627d07ae34a4e2846d67/library/std/Cargo.toml) gates `fortanix-sgx-abi` to the SGX target and its direct `r-efi` dependencies to UEFI; these names do not appear in the ARM64 musl link map. The same Rust revision’s [root lockfile](https://github.com/rust-lang/rust/blob/58cf8f9c35b355199727627d07ae34a4e2846d67/Cargo.lock) also records `getrandom 0.4.2 → r-efi 6.0.0`. The official `getrandom 0.4.2` archive matched its locked SHA-256 `0de51e6874e94e7bf76d726fc5d13ba782deca734ff60d5bb2fb2607c7406555`; its manifest gates that edge on UEFI with the `efi_rng` backend. This identifies one target-gated route, not necessarily every route by which the broad copyright inventory includes that entry. Possible inlining and other source inclusions are not resolved solely by name absence. Do not treat the broad inventory as proof of linkage, or absence from the map as proof of absence.
